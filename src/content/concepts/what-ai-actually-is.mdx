## One idea explains the rest

Almost every confusing thing about AI stops being confusing once a child understands that AI predicts things rather
than knows things.

Confident wrong answers make sense. Different answers to the same question make
sense. The inability to say "I am not sure" makes sense. All of it follows from
the same mechanism, and a kid who has that mechanism does not need to memorize a
list of warnings.

## Do not oversell either direction

Be careful with how you frame the description.

Telling a child AI is magic leaves them unable to evaluate anything it says.
Telling them it is junk leaves them unprepared for a tool they will use
constantly and which is, in fact, extremely capable at some things.

The accurate version is stranger and more interesting than either: it is a
remarkable machine with a specific mechanism and specific failure modes, built by
companies with their own reasons, and we do not entirely understand it. Children
handle that better than adults expect.
