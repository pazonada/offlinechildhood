## Lead with permanence

The instinct is to teach this through fear: strangers, predators, ruined futures.
It backfires twice. It frightens children about the rarest outcomes, and it makes
them hide the ordinary problems, which are the ones you can actually help with.

Permanence works better and happens to be more accurate. Things last. They travel.
You cannot reliably take them back. That is a rule about how the world works
rather than a warning about villains, and children apply rules more readily than
they apply fears.

## Start with your own behaviour

This is the concept where adults are least credible.

A child who has been photographed and posted for a decade, without ever being
asked, will notice the inconsistency the moment you raise this. Asking her
permission before you post, and honouring a refusal, is worth more than the whole
conversation. It also gives her a script she can use with her own friends, which
is where most of this actually plays out.
