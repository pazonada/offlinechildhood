      Look at the watch history or search history on a service you both use. The
      length of it is the point. She will be surprised, which is the correct
      reaction.
    listenFor: >-
      Her asking who can see it. That question is exactly right and leads
      naturally to the next band.
    ifMoreExperienced: >-
      Show her how to clear a history, and be honest that clearing the view is not

  - band: '10-12'
    explain: >-
      Introduce inference, which is the part that surprises people. "The
      interesting thing is not the facts you typed in. It is what can be worked
      out from your behaviour. When you are awake. Where you go. Who you spend
      time with. What you paused on. From that you can be sorted into categories
      you never chose and would probably not agree with."
    tryThis: >-
      Look together at the inferred-interests page in a service she uses, which
      most large platforms provide. The wrong guesses are as instructive as the
      right ones, because both show a profile being built without her.
    listenFor: >-
      Her distinguishing what she told them from what they worked out. That
      distinction is the concept.

      Introduce the idea that this data is bought and sold between companies she

    explain: >-
      Extend to permanence and consequence. Data collected now persists into a

## Permanence, not danger

The instinct is to teach this through fear: strangers, predators, ruined futures.
It backfires twice. It frightens children about the rarest outcomes, and it makes
them hide the ordinary problems, which are the ones you can actually help with.

Permanence works better and happens to be more accurate. Things last. They travel.
You cannot reliably take them back. That is a rule about how the world works
rather than a warning about villains, and children apply rules more readily than
they apply fears.

## Start with your own behaviour

This is the concept where adults are least credible.

A child who has been photographed and posted for a decade, without ever being
asked, will notice the inconsistency the moment you raise this. Asking her
permission before you post, and honouring a refusal, is worth more than the whole
conversation. It also gives her a script she can use with her own friends, which
is where most of this actually plays out.
