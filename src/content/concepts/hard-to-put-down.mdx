## Say it is not their fault, and mean it

The most valuable sentence in this whole concept is that the difficulty is not a
personal failing.

Children are told constantly to have more self-control about screens, and they
mostly cannot, because the thing was built by well-resourced teams specifically
to defeat that. Being told to try harder against a professional adversary you
cannot see is demoralising, and it produces concealment sooner than restraint.

Naming the design does something different. It makes the difficulty external,
which makes it discussable, which is the only route to a kid telling you when
something has got out of hand.

## Then change the environment

Once the mechanism is named, skip the willpower and change the setup: fewer
prompts, more friction. Notifications off, phone in another room, the app
removed from the home screen.

This is also why deciding [where devices live](/decisions/where-devices-live)
does more work than any rule about minutes. It changes the environment rather
than asking a child to win a fight nobody wins.
