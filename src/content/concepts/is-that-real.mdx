## The habit outlasts the tells

Every article about spotting fakes lists visual giveaways. Those lists are
obsolete within months, and worse, they leave a child confident in a skill that stopped working once the tech
improved.

The durable skill is understanding the content's origin. Ask: where did this
come from? Who first published it? Is anyone reliable saying the same thing?
Who gains if I believe it?

Those questions worked before synthetic media existed and will keep working
after, because they are about the chain of custody rather than the pixels.

## Do not land on total distrust

You don't want a teenager who concludes that nothing can be known and therefore
nothing is worth checking.

The goal is a child who can understand that fakery is cheap and common, that
some sources are much more reliable than others, and that telling them apart is
work worth doing.
