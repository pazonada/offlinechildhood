## Teach the habit, not the tells

Every article about spotting fakes lists visual giveaways. Those lists are
obsolete within months, and worse, they leave a child confident in a skill that
has quietly stopped working.

The durable skill is provenance. Where did this come from? Who first published
it? Is anyone reliable saying the same thing? Who gains if I believe it?

Those questions worked before synthetic media existed and will keep working
after, because they are about the chain of custody rather than the pixels.

## Do not land on total distrust

The failure mode is a teenager who concludes that nothing can be known and
therefore nothing is worth checking. That is more useful to a liar than
credulity, and it is where a lot of "do your own research" ends up.

The goal is a child who can hold two things at once: that fakery is cheap and
common, and that some sources are much more reliable than others, and that
telling them apart is work worth doing.
