People we read

Being listed here is not an endorsement of everything someone argues. The list deliberately includes people who disagree with each other, because on this subject the disagreement is the most useful thing to understand. Each link goes to where they actually publish.

  • Abby

    Speech language pathologist

    On the role of play in language development, including gestalt language processors, and on building responsible screen habits early. Her feed is the main channel and carries most of the resources.

    Read AbbyInstagram for Abby

  • Ryan Broderick

    Writes Garbage Day

    A journalist on internet culture, which is the water your kids are swimming in. The best way to understand the gaming and meme worlds they are joining, told with more affection than alarm.

    Read Ryan BroderickInstagram for Ryan BroderickX for Ryan Broderick

  • Mariana Brussoni

    Professor, University of British Columbia

    Leads the research on risky play that position statements cite. Built OutsidePlay, a free tool that walks a worried parent through letting go a little, which is rarer and more useful than another warning.

    Read Mariana Brussoni

  • Raffaele Ciriello

    University of Sydney

    Writes on human and AI companionship, which is becoming one of the harder things for parents to think clearly about, and on the ethics of emerging technology.

    Read Raffaele CirielloLinkedIn for Raffaele Ciriello

  • De Kai

    Professor and author, The Future Society

    An AI researcher writing about what these systems do to the societies using them, beyond what they can do.

    Read De Kai

  • Peter Gray

    Research professor, Boston College

    The psychologist of free play. Decades of evidence that children build judgement and courage in play adults stay out of, written up weekly on his Substack with patience and heat.

    Read Peter Gray

  • Sam Gregory

    Researcher on deepfakes and media authenticity

    Deepfakes, synthetic media and how anyone can tell what is real. He helped write the technical standard for signalling where an image came from, and argues for preparation without the panic.

    Read Sam GregoryLinkedIn for Sam Gregory

  • Jonathan Haidt

    Writes After Babel

    Argues that a play-based childhood gave way to a phone-based one. The most influential case for restriction, and the most contested. He publishes the argument, and the pushback to it, here rather than in books alone.

    Read Jonathan HaidtInstagram for Jonathan Haidt

  • Tristan Harris

    Co-founder, Center for Humane Technology

    How products are designed to hold attention, explained clearly enough to repeat to a child. Their podcast, Your Undivided Attention, is the easiest way in.

    Read Tristan HarrisInstagram for Tristan Harris

  • Christine Haskell

    Technology advisor and author

    On data practice and technology leadership, and runs workshops. Useful if you are the person raising this at a school or a workplace.

    Read Christine Haskell

  • Na Young Kim

    Lawyer and data scientist

    Advises on AI governance and cybersecurity, which is the layer most parenting coverage skips entirely.

    Read Na Young KimLinkedIn for Na Young Kim

  • Nataliya Kos'myna

    Research scientist, MIT Media Lab

    Works on the interaction between AI and the brain, including attention and learning, with UNICEF and Google.

    Read Nataliya Kos'myna

  • Sonia Livingstone

    Professor, LSE

    Has studied children's online lives longer and across more countries than anyone. The evidence-first voice in a debate that runs hot on both sides, and the person to read when a headline seems too tidy.

    Read Sonia Livingstone

  • J. Nathan Matias

    Cornell, Citizens and Technology Lab

    Studies how platforms are governed and runs research that communities can actually take part in, rather than be the subject of.

    Read J. Nathan MatiasLinkedIn for J. Nathan Matias

  • Jacqueline Nesi

    Psychologist, Brown University

    Reads the studies before summarising them. Her Techno Sapiens newsletter is weekly, practical and allergic to panic, and is the closest thing this field has to a fair referee.

    Read Jacqueline Nesi

  • Catherine Price

    Writes How to Feel Alive

    Practical habit change for adults, which is where most family screen problems actually start. She runs a phone breakup challenge each February that is worth doing yourself before asking it of your kids.

    Read Catherine PriceInstagram for Catherine Price

  • Lenore Skenazy

    Founder, Let Grow

    Made the case for free-range childhood before it had a movement. Let Grow turns the argument into school programmes and a defence of ordinary independence, and it is the practical arm of everything this site says about play.

    Read Lenore Skenazy

  • Science editorial desk

    Peer-reviewed journal

    Useful counterweight on how unsettled the social media research actually is. Worth reading next to Haidt, as the cross-examination.

    Read Science editorial desk

  • Alison Yeung

    Writes The Smartphone Effect MD

    A doctor and a mother, writing weekly on helping parents push back against a tech-driven culture. Practical toolkits, light on theory.

    Read Alison YeungInstagram for Alison Yeung
