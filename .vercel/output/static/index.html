Childhood in the real world

Our culture is saturated with screens, but there's a growing movement toward more analog ways of living: more play, more paper, more outside time. And because technology is unavoidable for this generation, media literacy is critical for adults and kids to understand how these technologies actually work, who owns the platforms, and how to navigate them safely and responsibly.

We argue from what a childhood gains

Whether phones are behind the rise in teenage anxiety is still genuinely disputed among researchers, and we will not pretend otherwise. We do not need that argument. The case for analog stands on firmer ground, starting with arithmetic: an hour on a screen is an hour spent on something else.

  • Paper beats screen

    Across 39 studies of about 1,800 young children, comprehension was lower from a digital book than from the same book on paper.

    Furenes, Kucirkova & Bus, Review of Educational Research, 2021

  • It is the portable ones

    When researchers asked which screens actually cost children sleep, the answer was the devices that follow a kid into the bedroom.

    Twenge, Hisler & Krizan, Sleep Medicine, 2019

  • Play compounds

    Unstructured free play in the early years predicted stronger self-regulation years later, and that capacity tracks with outcomes across a lifetime.

    Colliver, Harrison, Brown & Humburg, Early Childhood Research Quarterly, 2022
