What to buy

A short list, kept short on purpose. Every entry says what it is bad at, because a recommendation with no stated downside is an advert. Some links pay us a commission, and those are labelled.

Read the decision first

This page is deliberately the least important one on the site. Work out what you want to do in Decisions, then come back if a purchase turns out to be part of the answer. Often it is not.

Phones and watches

Gabb Watch

Gabb

$$

A wrist device for calls and location to approved contacts, which solves the logistics problem most families are actually trying to solve.

What it isn't good at. Requires a monthly plan. Location tracking is worth thinking about carefully, because a habit of constant visibility is hard to unwind later.

Light Phone III

Light

$$$

A phone that makes calls and sends texts and does close to nothing else, for a teenager who genuinely needs to be reachable.

What it isn't good at. Expensive for what it does, and the lack of a group-chat experience is a real social cost for a kid whose friends coordinate that way. Maps and music exist but are deliberately basic.

Audio and stories

Toniebox

tonies

$$

The same idea as Yoto for younger children, with figures instead of cards, which suits hands that cannot yet read labels.

What it isn't good at. Figures are licensed and pricey, and the catalogue leans heavily on brand tie-ins. Best paired with a library card.

Visit tonies(opens in a new tab)Paid link

Yoto Player

Yoto

$$

Audio without a screen, controlled by physical cards a child can choose from a shelf, which keeps the choosing analog.

What it isn't good at. The card library adds up fast. The device is a screen-free win, but it still creates a small collecting habit worth watching.

Visit Yoto(opens in a new tab)Paid link

Cameras and making

Instax Mini

Fujifilm

$$

A camera that prints, so a photograph becomes an object on a wall instead of the ten-thousandth file in a roll nobody opens.

What it isn't good at. Film is a running cost and it adds up quickly with an enthusiastic seven-year-old. Buy film in bulk and set a per-week limit before it becomes a source of conflict.

Software and settings

Daylight Computer

Daylight

$$$

A tablet with a paper-like display and no colour video, aimed at reading and writing rather than watching.

What it isn't good at. Costs as much as a laptop and is a niche purchase. For most families a library card and a paperback do the same job for nothing.
