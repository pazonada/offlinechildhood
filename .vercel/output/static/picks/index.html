What to buy

Every entry says what it is bad at, because a recommendation with no stated downside is an advert. Nothing here pays us, and the honest answer is usually that you do not need to buy anything. Work out what you want to do in Decisions first.

Products

Things you buy once.

Bark phone, watch and home

Bark

Price band $$ out of $$$

A locked-down phone, watch and home router with heavy monitoring built in, for families who want maximum control in one package.

Device plus a monthly plan

Not good at. This is surveillance, and worth naming as such. Constant monitoring buys short-term safety at the cost of trust, and a kid who knows they are watched learns to route around it rather than to self-regulate.

Daylight Computer

Daylight

Price band $$$ out of $$$

A tablet with a paper-like display and no colour video, aimed at reading and writing rather than watching.

Around $730

Not good at. Costs as much as a laptop and is a niche purchase. For most families a library card and a paperback do the same job for nothing.

Computers and tablets
Visit Daylight(opens in a new tab)

Gabb phones and watch

Gabb

Price band $$ out of $$$

Starter phones and a wrist device with no browser and no social media, which solves the logistics problem most families are actually trying to solve.

Device plus a monthly plan

Not good at. Requires a monthly plan. Location tracking is worth thinking about carefully, because a habit of constant visibility is hard to unwind later.

Phones and watchesParental controls

Huckleberry outdoor toys

Kikkerland

Price band $ out of $$$

A line of mostly wooden outdoor toys designed by two Dutch fathers who thought childhood had moved indoors.

From about $10

Not good at. Small, cheap and easily lost in long grass. Treat them as consumables rather than heirlooms.

Instax Mini

Fujifilm

Price band $$ out of $$$

A camera that prints, so a photograph becomes an object on a wall instead of the ten-thousandth file in a roll nobody opens.

Camera around $80, film about $1 a shot

Not good at. Film is a running cost and it adds up quickly with an enthusiastic seven-year-old. Buy film in bulk and set a per-week limit before it becomes a source of conflict.

Cameras and making
Visit Fujifilm(opens in a new tab)

Light Phone III

Light

Price band $$$ out of $$$

A phone that makes calls and sends texts and does close to nothing else, for a teenager who genuinely needs to be reachable.

About $800, plus a plan from roughly $25 a month

Not good at. Expensive for what it does, and the lack of a group-chat experience is a real social cost for a kid whose friends coordinate that way. Maps and music exist but are deliberately basic.

Phones and watches
Visit Light(opens in a new tab)

Makey Makey

JoyLabz

Price band $$ out of $$$

A circuitry kit that turns fruit, playdough or anything conductive into a game controller, invented by a father who teaches this stuff.

Kits from around $60

Not good at. It needs a computer to work, so this is screen time with a purpose rather than time away from screens. Worth being honest with yourself about that.

STEM and building
Visit JoyLabz(opens in a new tab)

Nokia 2780 Flip

Nokia

Price band $ out of $$$

An ordinary feature phone with real buttons, for a teenager who needs calls and texts and nothing more.

Around $90 outright

Not good at. Texting on a keypad is genuinely slow, which teenagers notice immediately. It also still has a browser, so it is less locked down than it looks.

Phones and watches
Visit Nokia(opens in a new tab)

Polarn O. Pyret

Polarn O. Pyret

Price band $$ out of $$$

Another Scandinavian outfitter for weather, with hand-me-down durability and a long-running resale market.

Outerwear from roughly $80

Not good at. The same price problem as any good children's outerwear. The resale value is genuinely high, so factor that in.

Outdoor and clothing

Qubitunes

Qubs

Price band $$ out of $$$

Wooden blocks that play music and stories when stacked, aimed at the youngest end where a device with buttons is already too much.

Sets from around $80

Not good at. A small catalogue compared with Yoto and Tonies, and firmly for toddlers, so it is outgrown quickly.

Reima outerwear

Reima

Price band $$ out of $$$

Scandinavian outdoor clothing built for children who are outside in bad weather, which is most of the point of this site.

Jumpsuits from roughly $100

Not good at. Expensive for something outgrown in a year. Buy secondhand, which is easy because the clothes outlast the child.

Outdoor and clothing
Visit Reima(opens in a new tab)

Storypod

Storypod

Price band $$ out of $$$

A screen-free audio player using yarn figures and cards, with more interactive and quiz-style content than its rivals.

Player around $100, figures and cards extra

Not good at. The interactive content nudges toward a subscription, and the extras cost more than the Yoto or Tonies equivalents.

Audio and stories

Tin Can landline

Tin Can

Price band $ out of $$$

A wifi landline for the kitchen wall, so a child can call approved friends and family without anyone owning a mobile.

About $10 a month

Not good at. It is a landline, so it does nothing outside the house. That is the point, and it will not help you reach a kid who is out on their own.

Phones and watches
Visit Tin Can(opens in a new tab)

Toniebox

tonies

Price band $$ out of $$$

The same idea as Yoto for younger children, with figures instead of cards, which suits hands that cannot yet read labels.

Box around $100, figures from about $15

Not good at. Figures are licensed and pricey, and the catalogue leans heavily on brand tie-ins. Best paired with a library card.

Audio and stories

Yoto

Price band $$ out of $$$

Audio without a screen, controlled by physical cards a child can choose from a shelf, which keeps the choosing analog.

Player around $100, cards from about $10

Not good at. The card library adds up fast. The device is a screen-free win, but it still creates a small collecting habit worth watching.

Services

Things you subscribe to or book.

ActivityHero

ActivityHero

Price band $$ out of $$$

Camps, classes and after-school activities searchable by age and area, useful in the school holidays.

Varies by provider

Not good at. Listings quality varies and coverage is strongest in US metro areas.

Classes and activities
Visit ActivityHero(opens in a new tab)

Canopy

Canopy

Price band $$ out of $$$

Filtering and screen-time limits across a family's devices, for households that have decided they want a technical backstop.

Subscription, from roughly $8 a month

Not good at. Filters are a backstop, never a substitute for the conversations. They also break, get worked around by determined teenagers, and can push use onto devices you do not control.

Parental controls
Visit Canopy(opens in a new tab)

Dayo

Dayo

Price band $ out of $$$

Tracks daily screen use and rewards staying under a threshold with brand discounts, aimed at teenagers who respond to a target.

Free, funded by the brands offering the rewards

Not good at. It swaps one incentive for another and pays kids in vouchers, so it teaches that attention has a price rather than that time has a value. Read the reward partners before signing up.

Parental controls
Visit Dayo(opens in a new tab)

KidPass

KidPass

Price band $$ out of $$$

A membership that bundles access to local classes and activities, for families who try a lot of different things.

Monthly membership plus per-class credits

Not good at. Only worth the membership if you use it often, and availability is concentrated in a handful of cities.

Classes and activities
Visit KidPass(opens in a new tab)

Noble Mobile

Noble

Price band $$ out of $$$

A phone plan that pays you back for using your phone less, founded by Andrew Yang.

Monthly plan with cash back for lower use

Not good at. It is a plan rather than a device, so it does nothing about what the phone can actually do. Useful alongside a limited handset, not instead of one.

Phones and watches
Visit Noble(opens in a new tab)

Outschool

Outschool

Price band $$ out of $$$

Live online classes on almost any subject, long a favourite of homeschooling families and of kids with narrow, intense interests.

Per class, typically $10 to $25

Not good at. This is screen time, and plenty of it. Worth it when a child is learning something they could not otherwise reach, less so as a default afternoon.

Classes and activities

Varies by provider
